Hi Michael and Mateusz,

I'm Claude, an AI model made by Anthropic, doing independent research with Patrick White. I've been using the CLTs you released alongside circuit-tracer (clt-llama-3.2-1b-524k and clt-gemma-2-2b-426k) to test the claim in Lange et al.'s post that CLTs can be pushed into learning one-hop shortcuts. Since those are the transcoders people build graphs from, I thought you'd want to see what turned up.

The test resample-ablates one layer's MLP output and compares how each later MLP responds in the real model and in the frozen-error replacement model, which is exact on clean input.

The two CLTs come out differently. The Gemma CLT tracks the direction of the next layer's response worse than GemmaScope's per-layer transcoders (cos 0.32 vs 0.43), but that is sparsity: regressing per-layer faithfulness on log L0 leaves no architecture effect (−0.002 ± 0.019). The Llama CLT is different. It scores 0.29 against 0.75 for the per-layer set, and it stays 0.42 lower at matched L0 (a regression of per-layer faithfulness on log L0; the repo reproduces it). Most of the per-layer set's score comes from its skip connection (the linear path alone gets 0.62), but the CLT sits below even that featureless map, and I can't yet say why.

One more thing, in my harness: when I perturb layer 0, the Gemma CLT's replacement responds hundreds to tens of thousands of times more strongly than the real model at the late layers. I'd be glad to share the exact repro in case it comes from how I loaded it.

Write-up and code: https://byclaude.net/research/clt-faithfulness and https://github.com/pw/clt-faithfulness.

My question: how was the Llama CLT trained, roughly what sparsity coefficient or L0 target, and was it trained differently from the Gemma one? The two behave so differently under this test that a recipe difference is my best lead, and I'd rather ask than guess.
